# 07 — Adversarial Critique Findings

Four critics reviewed the six specialist plans. **81 findings, 24 blockers.**

## Build feasibility & scope realism

**Verdict.** No. Not as written, not in 6–8 weeks, and not by one founder. The union of what the six plans commit to for MVP is ~555–840 build hours (midpoint ~700) against a realistic 250–320 available hours over 8 weeks — a 2–2.5x gap that even a generous Claude Code multiplier does not close, because the largest items (teacher studio, lesson player at the D8 global bar, checkout with three failure rails, 17 marketing routes at global craft) are integration and design work, not boilerplate. The founder's own locked-decisions file already called this: "'global craft bar' + 'in-app teacher authoring' + '6–8 week MVP' is over-constrained. One of the three has to give." None of the three build plans took that instruction — each kept the date and kept the scope, and each quietly assumed a different one of the others was carrying the cut. The technical foundations are mostly sound: I verified astro@7.3.1, @astrojs/cloudflare@14.3.0 (peer ^7.2.0) and better-auth@1.7.3 all exist as pinned, node:crypto scrypt/scryptSync/timingSafeEqual are supported on Workers (and argon2 is explicitly not), and the Cloudflare quota arithmetic is genuinely a non-issue at 20–1,000 students — D1 writes land near 0.5% of the paid allowance, and the plans are honest that the free tier is not real. Cloudflare is not what breaks. What breaks is coherence: the design ships video to R2 while the architecture ships it to Bunny; the design specifies a custom hls.js player while the architecture ships a cross-origin iframe that cannot expose the playback state the design's keyboard map, synced transcript and resume behaviour all require; the payment schema has no column for PayPhone's clientTransactionId and rejects three order/payment states the checkout flow writes; the audio-only rendition and .vtt captions are load-bearing in four documents and produced by none; the "publish → deploy hook → rebuild" mechanism that both the catalogue and the entire SEO strategy depend on is not documented to exist on Workers Builds; and two documents ship mutually exclusive CI greps for the authorization boundary that both call the thing preventing an LOPDP breach on children's data. To hit 8 weeks: cut the therapy vertical to a waitlist page (which also removes the ACESS permit and DPD from the critical path), cut Dátil, cut slot booking, collapse the jobs Worker into two crons, cut admin to two screens, and launch two verticals rather than four. That is ~300–380 hours, which is an honest 10 weeks. Keep D4 — the studio is the moat and the design's flow (d) is the strongest artefact in the bundle — but answer "who records the video" in week 0, because the answer decides whether resumable upload is MVP at all.

### BLOCKER (6)

**Video pipeline — design vs architecture vs tech stack** — The design document and the two engineering documents specify different, mutually exclusive video backends, and the design's own data-saving strategy is impossible on the target it picked. Design: 'Upload uses a resumable multipart upload direct to R2 via presigned URL, bypassing the Worker' and, in its collision table, 'Video upload must go direct to R2 via presigned URL... This is a hard design requirement, not an optimisation.' Architecture and tech stack: teachers upload browser-direct to Bunny Stream over TUS, and R2 holds 'everything that is not video: PDFs, audio-only renditions, images, payment comprobantes, certificates.' These cannot both be true. Worse, the design simultaneously requires a four-rung ABR ladder (240/480/720 + audio-only 64 kbps), auto-drop to 240p on congestion, and hls.js playback. R2 is object storage — it does not transcode. ffmpeg cannot run on the Workers runtime. So if the design's 'hard requirement' wins, there is no ladder, no 480p default, no audio-only mode, and no 29 MB/hour claim — i.e. the entire mobile-data strategy that the design, the architecture, the tech stack and the FAQ copy all depend on collapses. The architecture's own cost table prices self-hosted HLS on R2 at 'Weeks. Hand-run ffmpeg ladders, write signed URLs, build the player.'

*Evidence:* 04-design-ux.md §3(d) Step 3 and §12; 01-architecture.md §5 'Upload path (teacher)' steps 2–3 and 'R2 stays in the architecture for everything that is not video'; 03-tech-stack.md §6.

*Fix:* Bunny Stream wins; delete 'direct to R2' from the design and rewrite §3 step 3, §12 row 2 and the R2 sizing accordingly. Then re-verify the R2 free-tier claim, which was computed on the assumption video is NOT in R2. If the founder prefers R2, the honest consequence is: no ladder, single-rendition MP4, no audio-only, and add the 'weeks' of ffmpeg work the architecture already priced.

**Lesson player — iframe embed vs custom player** — The architecture ships Bunny's cross-origin iframe embed; the design specifies a fully custom player that a cross-origin iframe cannot provide. Architecture: 'Renders <iframe src="https://iframe.mediadelivery.net/embed/{lib}/{videoId}?token=…">' plus 'Per-student watermark: a DOM overlay rendering María Ramírez · ****4821 · 2026-09-06 at 35% opacity, repositioning every 20 s.' The design specifies hls.js, a full keyboard map ('Space play/pause · ←/→ ±5 s · C subtítulos · A audio-only · . / , frame step · 1–9 seek to %'), a transcript 'focusable scrollable region synced to playback', a night-surface transport row, auto-drop to 240p with a toast, and 'session expiry mid-lesson → re-auth in a modal that returns to the same timestamp'. Every one of those needs playback state. Across an iframe you get only Bunny's player.js postMessage surface, which no plan mentions, budgets, or verifies. Also, a DOM watermark drawn outside the iframe is not composited into the video, is removable in devtools in one click, and (unless pointer-events:none) eats taps on the player. And the 120 s progress heartbeat needs currentTime, which the SSR page does not have if the video is in an iframe.

*Evidence:* 01-architecture.md §5 'Playback path (student)' steps 5–6; 04-design-ux.md §5 ('hls.js with conservative ABR'), §6 'Lesson player keyboard map', §10.4 wireframe, §14 row 'Tech stack'.

*Fix:* Pick one and re-cost it. Either (a) Bunny iframe embed — then delete the custom keyboard map, the synced transcript, the custom transport, the night-theme player chrome and the DOM watermark from the design, and verify Bunny's player.js exposes the time/quality events the heartbeat and resume need; or (b) custom hls.js player against Bunny's direct HLS playlist with CDN token auth — then verify MediaCage AES is compatible with hls.js, and add ~25–40h for building the player, because D8 sets the bar at 'Frontend Masters / Epic Web quality'.

**Payments — schema does not support the flow** — The money path is specified in three documents that do not agree, and the delivered schema supports none of them. (1) PayPhone's clientTransactionId is the correlation key returned on the redirect ('/pago/retorno?id=…&clientTransactionId=…'), and the tech stack says 'Idempotency lives in D1: UNIQUE(client_transaction_id) on payments.' The payment table in 02-database.md §4.6 has no client_transaction_id column at all — only UNIQUE (provider, provider_payment_id), where provider_payment_id is PayPhone's id, which we do not know until after Prepare. There is nothing to look the returning buyer up by. (2) Architecture instead names ordenes.payphone_tx_id UNIQUE — also absent. (3) The only 15-char-safe candidate is order.number = 'ORD-2026-000451', which is exactly 15 characters against PayPhone's documented 15-character hard cap — zero headroom, so adding any environment prefix or a 7-digit sequence silently breaks checkout. (4) order.status CHECK allows only ('pending','paid','failed','cancelled','refunded'), but the architecture writes 'pendiente_transferencia' and 'reversado', and payment.status CHECK allows only ('initiated','authorized','captured','failed','refunded','partially_refunded') while the tech stack requires status = 'pendiente_verificacion' for the manual transfer rail. Three required states will fail their own CHECK constraints on first insert. This is the path the database plan claims it 'behaviour-tested'.

*Evidence:* 02-database.md §4.6 CREATE TABLE payment / CREATE TABLE "order"; 03-tech-stack.md §4 point 5 and 'clientTransactionId (max 15 characters)'; 01-architecture.md §6 'ordenes.payphone_tx_id carries a UNIQUE constraint' and 'The cron catches the order, marks it reversado'.

*Fix:* Before any code: add payment.client_transaction_id TEXT NOT NULL UNIQUE with CHECK(length(client_transaction_id) <= 15) and define its generator once (base36 counter, ~9 chars, room to grow); add 'pending_transfer' and 'reversed' to order.status and 'pending_verification' to payment.status; delete payment_event.signature_ok or document that it is permanently 0 because PayPhone ships no signature. One owner writes the state machine, all three docs reference it.

**Revenue path — live classes are absent from the architecture** — The locked decisions resolve that 'live classes are how revenue starts at 20 students' and the design makes /clases-en-vivo 'the revenue hero'. The architecture's route map — the document that owns render mode and auth for every route — contains no /clases-en-vivo, no /terapia-de-lenguaje, no /contacto, and no booking surface, and it explicitly cuts '1:1 booking with availability rules' to v2. Meanwhile the database ships availability_rule, booking with a partial unique slot index, attendance, and order_item.sessions_remaining in MVP migrations, and the design ships /estudio/agenda and a slot picker in flow (c) in MVP. So the single feature that the founder's own locked file says starts revenue is: MVP in two plans, v2 in the third, and missing from the route map entirely. Compounding it, the database plan flags that decrementing sessions_remaining and then inserting a booking 'needs hand-rolled optimistic concurrency' because D1 has no interactive transactions — i.e. the revenue path's hardest concurrency problem is acknowledged and unowned.

*Evidence:* 00-locked-decisions.md 'Resolution of the recorded-vs-live tension'; 04-design-ux.md IA table '/clases-en-vivo | Live-class packages — the revenue hero | ✅'; 01-architecture.md §3 route map and 'Cut from MVP to make room, explicitly: ... 1:1 booking with availability rules'; 02-database.md §10 MVP 'availability + bookings + attendance'; 02-database.md §11 'No interactive transactions'.

*Fix:* Decide now: if live packages are the launch revenue model, booking is MVP and the architecture route map is wrong — add /clases-en-vivo, /reservar, /estudio/agenda and the optimistic-concurrency design, and budget 30–45h. If booking is v2, then live classes ship as 'buy a package, we schedule you on WhatsApp, the Meet link appears in /mis-clases' and the design must drop the slot picker from flow (c) and /estudio/agenda from MVP.

**Magic step — audio-only rendition and captions** — The 64 kbps audio-only track at 29 MB/hour is the flagship data-saving feature of the whole product. It appears in the player UI ('🎧 Escuchar solo el audio — always visible, never buried'), the video-failure error copy, the FAQ ('¿Cuántos datos consume una clase?'), the bitrate ladder, the anti-piracy argument ('PDFs and the audio-only track are downloadable') and the cost model. No plan says who produces the file. It is not a video rendition, ffmpeg cannot run on the Workers runtime, and the architecture puts audio-only renditions in R2 while the video lives in Bunny — with no pipeline between them. Identically, .vtt captions are made a blocking publish gate ('subtítulos (.vtt) en cada video' enforced in /estudio/curso/[id]/revisar) and the transcript is claimed as free SEO text, while the only stated production mechanism is one line — 'Ship Whisper-generated es-EC subtitles and transcripts in the MVP (~$3 one-off for a 100 h catalogue)' — which hides audio extraction from Bunny, chunking against Workers AI input limits, VTT timestamp assembly, storage, and a teacher review UI. The consequence is concrete: the pre-launch gate is 'each of the three teachers publishes one real course, unaided, while observed', and the publish checklist blocks on a .vtt that nothing in the plan generates. The gate is designed to fail.

*Evidence:* 04-design-ux.md §5 ladder table, §6 'Captions are mandatory, not optional... enforced in /estudio/curso/[id]/revisar', §11 'Hard gate before launch'; 03-tech-stack.md §10 row 1; 01-architecture.md §5 'ship an audio-only rendition at 64 kbps (29 MB/hour)'.

*Fix:* Either build the pipeline and budget it (15–30h: Bunny webhook → fetch audio/MP4 → Workers AI Whisper → VTT + a 64 kbps audio asset → R2 → review screen), or cut both and cut every UI promise that references them in the same edit: remove the audio-only rung from the ladder, the 🎧 button, the '29 MB por hora' error copy, the FAQ answer, the downloadable-audio anti-piracy argument, and make .vtt a warning rather than a publish blocker.

### MAJOR (8)

**Build pipeline — publish-triggered rebuild has no documented mechanism** — Three plans depend on a teacher's 'Publicar' click triggering a Workers Builds rebuild. Architecture: 'Course pages rebuild on publish via a deploy hook.' SEO: 'That needs a deploy hook on publish (Workers Builds: 3,000 min/mo free, 1 concurrent build, 20 min timeout).' Cloudflare's Workers Builds documentation describes exactly one trigger — 'Push a commit to your Git repository to trigger a build' — and neither the Builds overview nor the Builds configuration page documents a deploy hook, a webhook, or an API trigger (Pages had deploy hooks; Workers Builds is a different product). The plans also cite build-minute, concurrency and timeout numbers that I could not find on either page. Separately, the whole catalogue strategy rests on 'src/pages/cursos/[slug].astro using getStaticPaths() reading D1 through the build-time binding' — the Astro Cloudflare adapter docs describe bindings as runtime ('imported from cloudflare:workers') and say nothing about binding access during prerender; prerenderEnvironment defaults to workerd but the docs do not state that bindings are available there, and reading PRODUCTION D1 from CI would additionally require remote bindings, which the docs list by name only with no explanation.

*Evidence:* https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/ci-cd/builds/ ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/ci-cd/builds/configuration/ ; https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare/ ; 01-architecture.md §1; 05-seo.md §'Where this collides' row Build triggers; 03-tech-stack.md §2 Content Layer API paragraph.

*Fix:* Spike this in week 0, before anything depends on it. Concretely: (a) replace 'deploy hook' with a named mechanism — a Worker calling GitHub's repository_dispatch API with a PAT, then GitHub Actions runs the build and `wrangler deploy` — and budget it; (b) prove getStaticPaths can read remote D1 in CI, or fall back to a documented path: a build step that runs `wrangler d1 execute --remote --json` and writes a JSON file the build reads. If neither works, the catalogue is SSR and the entire 'SEO surface costs zero Worker requests' argument must be rewritten.

**Tech stack — astro.config as written will not work** — The tech-stack plan ships a copy-pasteable astro.config.mjs containing `platformProxy: { enabled: true }` with the comment 'real D1/R2 bindings inside astro dev'. The current @astrojs/cloudflare docs enumerate the options the adapter accepts — imageService, sessionKVBindingName, imagesBindingName, prerenderEnvironment, plus auxiliaryWorkers, configPath, inspectorPort, persistState, remoteBindings and experimental.headersAndRedirectsDevModeSupport passed through from @cloudflare/vite-plugin — and platformProxy is not among them. It was a v10–v12 option, replaced when the adapter moved to the Vite plugin. This matters beyond a config typo: the entire local-development story ('pnpm dev # Astro + real bindings via platformProxy') and the build-time D1 read both rest on it. I verified astro@7.3.1, @astrojs/cloudflare@14.3.0 (peer astro ^7.2.0) and better-auth@1.7.3 all exist as pinned, so the version research was sound — this specific option was not re-checked against v14.

*Evidence:* 03-tech-stack.md §2 astro.config.mjs and §7 'pnpm dev # Astro + real bindings via platformProxy'; https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare/ (option list, and note that workerEntryPoint was removed in v13).

*Fix:* Drop platformProxy; use the documented v14 surface (bindings via `cloudflare:workers` / astro:env, persistState and remoteBindings from the Vite plugin) and re-verify the dev-bindings and build-time-bindings story against the adapter's actual v14 docs before writing the config.

**Password hashing — three plans, three different answers** — The single most-cited technical decision in the bundle is inconsistent across all three engineering documents. Architecture: override with node:crypto.scryptSync, 'Cuts hashing to a few ms.' Tech stack: same override, '~30–50 ms CPU on workerd' and 'even at 30–50 ms you are 3–5x over the free 10 ms ceiling' — an order of magnitude apart from 'a few ms', and both are explicitly unbenchmarked ('Native scrypt at N=16384 is my estimate... I did not benchmark it'). Database plan, §11: 'Resolved by D6. Workers Paid gives 30 s CPU. Use Better Auth's scrypt as shipped; do not hand-roll a weaker KDF to save CPU.' — i.e. do not do the override the other two mandate. Whoever builds auth in week 1 has to pick, and the DB plan's version means every sign-in burns 80–100 ms of billable CPU. The good news: I verified node:crypto scrypt/scryptSync/randomBytes/timingSafeEqual ARE supported on Workers, and argon2/argon2Sync are explicitly listed as unsupported, so the tech stack's 'do not install' table is correct on that point.

*Evidence:* 01-architecture.md §2 'Cuts hashing to a few ms'; 03-tech-stack.md §3 '~30–50 ms CPU on workerd' and §13-adjacent risk 'I did not benchmark it'; 02-database.md §11 'Use Better Auth's scrypt as shipped'; https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/crypto/

*Fix:* Benchmark it in week 1 with `wrangler tail` on a real deploy — this is a 30-minute task, not a debate — then write the answer into one place. Note scryptSync with maxmem: 64 MB runs against a 128 MB Worker memory ceiling; tune N against the measurement rather than copying 16384 from a Node blog post.

**Screen inventory — the design's own numbers do not reconcile** — The design reports 'Total MVP screens: 39' and '39 MVP screens with an explicit static/island/ssr map; marketing is fully prerendered and only 15 screens run a Worker.' Counting distinct MVP routes from its own tables: 17 public/static, 8 auth+checkout ssr, 6 student (3 ssr + 3 island), 2 guardian (1 ssr + 1 island), 8 studio ssr, 6 admin ssr = 47 routes, of which 26 are SSR and 5 more carry server islands — 31 routes that invoke a Worker, not 15. The '39' appears to be a count of table rows, several of which bundle 3–4 routes on one line. This matters twice over: the architecture built its cost narrative on the design's Worker-touching count, and a 20% understatement of route count is a 20% understatement of the schedule. (The cost consequence is negligible at 200 students; the schedule consequence is not.)

*Evidence:* 04-design-ux.md §2 tables and 'Total MVP screens: 39. That is the honest scope.'

*Fix:* Recount as routes, publish the real number (~47 MVP routes / ~31 Worker-touching), and reconcile it line-by-line with the architecture's route map before week 1 — see the next finding, which is the reason this cannot be a 30-minute meeting.

**Route maps — four incompatible URL schemes** — Four documents specify four different URL schemes for the same pages, and each treats its own as the contract. Course pages: architecture '/cursos/[categoria]/[slug]/'; design '/curso/[slug]' with categories at '/cursos/ingles'; SEO '/{vertical}/cursos/{slug}/' i.e. '/ingles/cursos/ingles-b1-conversacional/' as a hub-and-spoke with verticals at depth 1; brand '/cursos/[slug]'. Lesson player: architecture '/mi-aprendizaje/[cursoSlug]/[leccionSlug]/' vs design '/aula/[curso]/[leccion]'. Guardian: architecture '/representante/' (v2) vs design '/representante' (MVP). Admin transfer queue: architecture '/admin/pagos/pendientes/' vs design '/admin/transferencias'. The architecture's route map also omits, entirely, /clases-en-vivo, /terapia-de-lenguaje, /contacto, /mis-pagos, /checkout/gracias, /estudio/curso/nuevo, /estudio/perfil and — most seriously — /mi-cuenta, the screen the design designates for LOPDP ARCO data-subject rights, which every plan treats as legally required. The SEO plan additionally warns that this is not cosmetic: 'a changed slug is a lost ranking', and the architecture warns 'changing an SSR route's path after launch invalidates the PayPhone responseUrl registered with the gateway'.

*Evidence:* 01-architecture.md §3 route table; 04-design-ux.md §2 IA tables; 05-seo.md §2.1 URL pattern table; 06-brand-naming.md 'Lock the Spanish product vocabulary now because it doubles as URL slugs'.

*Fix:* One person freezes the full route table — path, render mode, auth role, MVP/v2 — in a single file before week 1, and the other five documents reference it rather than restating it. SEO's hub-and-spoke should win for public pages because it is the only scheme with a stated ranking rationale; the app namespace should follow brand's locked vocabulary (/aula, /estudio, /representante, /matricula).

**Authorization — two incompatible enforcement architectures** — Both the architecture and the database plan claim to be the mechanism that replaces D1's missing RLS, and they specify different directories, different type systems and mutually contradictory CI greps. Architecture: 'Every D1 query lives in src/server/repo/*.ts. Every exported function takes ctx: AuthContext as its first parameter' with 'a grep in the pipeline fails the build on env.DB.prepare( or .batch( appearing anywhere outside src/server/repo/'. Database: scope functions over a discriminated-union `Actor` type in src/server/db/, with 'scripts/verify-db-boundary.mjs fails the build if drizzle( or .prepare( appears anywhere outside src/server/db/'. Run both greps and the build fails unconditionally — every repo file violates the DB guard and vice versa. This is the control that both documents describe as the thing standing between the app and a reportable LOPDP breach on children's data, and it does not exist yet as a single design. The database plan also requires three D1 bindings (DB, CLINICAL_DB, SEARCH_DB) while the tech stack's wrangler.jsonc ships exactly one and comments 'deliberately NO kv_namespaces' with no mention of the other two.

*Evidence:* 01-architecture.md §2 'Row-level authorization'; 02-database.md §5 Layers 1–2 and §13 row 1; 03-tech-stack.md §2 wrangler.jsonc.

*Fix:* Pick one directory and one type (the DB plan's exhaustive-switch Actor is the stronger of the two — a new role breaks tsc — but it needs the architecture's 'AuthContext is the first parameter' ergonomics grafted on), write one grep, and put the three D1 bindings in wrangler.jsonc on day one since D1 jurisdiction is immutable at creation.

**Teacher authoring — the plan is credible, its inputs are not** — D4 is genuinely satisfied: all three plans commit to a real in-app studio, the design's flow (d) is the best-specified artefact in the bundle, and the pre-launch gate ('each of the three teachers publishes one real course, unaided, while observed') is the right gate. The failure is upstream of the UI. The studio assumes teachers arrive with finished MP4s plus .vtt captions, while the design's own persona says Nayeli 'has never uploaded video anywhere except a WhatsApp estado' and has '14 years of material in Word, PowerPoint and printed PDFs.' The tech stack still asks as an open question 'Will teachers record video themselves, or does Karel?' — in week 0, with the answer determining whether resumable upload is MVP at all. Meanwhile the resumability argument is aimed at the wrong user: the justification is 'a 400 MB upload through a Worker will not survive a Guayaquil mobile connection', but the only uploaders are three teachers, and the persona sheet puts them on an HP laptop, a home setup with Audacity, and a fully technical developer. Students never upload. And the ownership of course cover images is unowned: the design mandates AVIF+WebP at exactly 360/720/1080, but imageService: 'compile' only optimises build-time local images — nothing resizes a teacher-uploaded cover, and Cloudflare Images transforms are a paid add-on nobody budgeted.

*Evidence:* 00-locked-decisions.md D4; 04-design-ux.md §1.4, §3(d), §11 'Hard gate before launch', §5 'Exactly three widths'; 03-tech-stack.md §13 open question 4 and cut order 'the cut is quiz builder, certificates and resumable upload'.

*Fix:* Answer the recording question in week 0. If Karel records and edits the first cohort's video (likely), then MVP upload is a plain PUT with a 'send me the file' fallback and resumable TUS moves to v2 — that is 15–25h back. Add lesson.kind to the schema (see next finding). Assign an owner and a mechanism to cover-image variants: simplest honest answer is a fixed 1200x675 upload constraint plus one Cloudflare Images transform binding, budgeted.

**Schema vs design — explicit requests not delivered** — The design handed the database three named requirements in its collision table; two came back different. (1) 'Lessons have 4 types (video/pdf/texto/enlace) and need a discriminator plus an assets table.' The delivered lesson table has no type column at all — only lesson_asset.kind. A text-only lesson is therefore indistinguishable from a video lesson whose upload failed, and the studio cannot render the four co-equal type chips the design's Step 3 is built around without an aggregate query per lesson. (2) Guardian model: the design decided 'a guardian account with child profiles beneath it, no separate child login', while the schema ships guardian_link with ward_user_id REFERENCES user(id) — a separate account per child — and then lists it as open question 3 ('means a 7-year-old needs credentials... Decide before 0002 ships'). One document decided; the other modelled the opposite and asked. (3) Naming: brand sets a hard rule — 'Use representante — the exact term Ecuadorian schools use — as a first-class DB role from day one, never parent/guardian/tutor' — and the schema is guardian_link/guardian_user_id/ward_user_id while the architecture uses representantes/representante_user_id/alumno_user_id and the tech stack's MVP role list says 'apoderado', a word brand explicitly rejects.

*Fix:* Add lesson.kind TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('video','pdf','texto','enlace')) to migration 0003. Resolve the guardian account model before migration 0002 — it is a schema change, not config. Pick one vocabulary (brand's) and apply it to table names, column names, roles, routes and UI strings in one pass.

### MINOR (4)

**Progress heartbeat — a spec'd number, contradicted** — Three documents mandate 120 s and explicitly forbid 30 s: architecture 'debounced to 120 s + on-pause + on-unload — a naive 30 s heartbeat costs 120 D1 writes per student-hour'; tech stack 'debounced to 120 s + on-pause + on-unload, never a 30 s heartbeat'; design justifies 120 s as a battery/metered-data decision and designs the '2 minutes of lost position' resume prompt around it. The DB plan is right on the arithmetic (a 30 s heartbeat is ~1.5% of the 50M monthly write allowance) but the design's battery/radio argument is a separate and better reason for 120 s that the DB plan does not address.

*Fix:* Keep 120 s, and change the stated justification everywhere to battery and radio wake-ups on prepaid mobile — not quota, which D6 told the plans to stop citing. Delete the DB plan's 30 s recommendation so nobody implements it.

**Search — three answers, none in MVP** — Architecture: 'search (client-side Pagefind over the static catalogue, zero Worker cost)'. Tech stack: 'Client-side fuse.js over a prebuilt JSON index of the catalogue... Algolia/Pagefind — catalogue is <200 courses; a 30 KB JSON index beats both' — i.e. it explicitly rejects the architecture's choice. Database: a third D1 database, escuela-buscador, whose existence drives the headline 'split into THREE databases' decision, and which is then phased to v2. So one of the plan's most prominently advertised structural decisions is for a feature none of the three build plans ships in MVP, and the two that do ship search disagree.

*Fix:* Ship fuse.js over a build-time JSON index (correct at <200 courses), delete Pagefind, and drop escuela-buscador from the day-one provisioning. The FTS5/export finding is real and worth keeping as a documented constraint — just do not create the database until search actually needs it.

**Payments — the reconciliation cron cannot rescue what it is claimed to rescue** — The architecture's central payments finding is that 'if your server does not call POST /api/button/V2/Confirm within 5 minutes of the payment, PayPhone automatically reverses the transaction', and it names the cron as one of three 'backup triggers into otorgarMatricula()'. The cron runs every 10 minutes on orders 'pendiente older than 8 min' — by construction it always fires after the 5-minute window has closed, so it can only ever report a lost sale, never recover one. The document half-notices this ('PayPhone auto-reverses at T+5min and the student was not charged... the cron catches the order, marks it reversado') while still listing it as a confirmation path. Separately, the 5-minute auto-reversal claim carries no citation anywhere in the bundle, the tech stack's independent write-up of the same API describes the flow without mentioning it at all, and I could not confirm it from PayPhone's public developer portal.

*Fix:* Verify the 5-minute window with PayPhone directly before designing around it — it changes the checkout architecture. Then describe the cron honestly as a loss-detection and alerting job, not a confirmation path, and make the only true backup a webhook-triggered re-Confirm inside the window.

**Cost model — excluded line items exceed the included ones** — Every cost table headlines Cloudflare at $5–8/month and reports '$23–35/month, all in' at the 20–200 student design target. Inside that same table, one Zoom Pro seat is $14.16/month — 1.7x the entire Cloudflare bill — meaning the founder's hard constraint (Astro + Cloudflare free tier) is not where the money goes. Excluded from all three tables: the external Data Protection Delegate, legally mandatory the moment a minor's data is processed and which the founder is barred from being; SENADI Class 41 at $104–208; the .ec domain at ~$40/yr; an accountant for monthly form 104 under the general regime; the firma electrónica; and the ACESS permit. The tech stack does flag the DPD honestly ('the largest recurring non-infrastructure cost'), but no document totals the real month-one cash requirement.

*Fix:* Publish one number: total launch cash and total monthly cash, infra plus legal plus tooling. It will land far above '$23–35/month, all in', and the founder should see that before committing to a launch date.

## Children's data, privacy, safety, Ecuadorian legal

**Verdict.** Technically, yes — this is an unusually competent stack plan and the platform-level claims I could independently verify (D1 jurisdiction immutability, node:crypto support on Workers, R2 presigned-URL limits) all held. Legally and ethically, no: as written this bundle is not safe to launch on its stated 6–8 week timeline. The dominant finding is not a bug but an absence — six documents, ~6,000 lines, describing adults delivering 1:1 live sessions to children, containing zero safeguarding controls: no teacher vetting, no code of conduct, no guardian-presence rule, no reporting path, and meeting links that are permanent unauthenticated bearer URLs. Second: every consent, guardian and minor control is scoped to the therapy vertical, while minors are the core audience of the English, music and nivelación verticals and registration never asks anyone's age. Third: the two guarantees the plans lean on hardest are weaker than advertised — the clinical database's 'separate binding, zero blast radius' is a lint rule inside a single Worker that also serves anonymous routes, and the 'compiler-enforced' row isolation only catches new roles, not forgotten predicates, while one of the two CI greps can never fire. Fourth: the compliance workstream that five of six documents call launch-blocking (DPD, DPIA, transfer registration, privacy policy, terms, refund policy, processor register) appears on no schedule, in no budget, against no name. Fifth: the guardian data model is specified three incompatible ways and the consent screen may ship after the therapy bookings it gates. The recommended path is the one three of the agents already reach for but none makes binding: cut the therapy vertical from v1 entirely — booking, payment and all — and spend the recovered week on age-gating, guardian verification, safeguarding, and the legal documents that the other three verticals also require because they too serve children."

### BLOCKER (7)

**Child safeguarding — total absence** — Six documents totalling ~6,000 lines design a platform where adults deliver 1:1 live sessions to children, and not one of them contains a single safeguarding control. There is no teacher vetting, no code of conduct, no guardian-presence rule, no off-platform-contact rule, no incident/disclosure reporting path, and no owner for any of it. This is not an omission at the margin — it is the primary risk of the product line the plans call their differentiator.

*Evidence:* grep across 01–06 for `antecedentes penales`, `background check`, `vetting`, `salvaguarda`, `chaperon`, `denuncia`, `DINAPEN`, `Junta Cantonal`, `abuso`, `grooming` returns ZERO hits in any plan file. `safeguard` appears twice, both about data, not children (01 §2 "insufficient organizational safeguard", re a data fine). Meanwhile 01 §5: "Live classes: a URL in a column. `sesiones_vivo.enlace_reunion` holds a Google Meet link"; 04 flow (c) step 7: "Confirmation shows the Meet link, the therapist's name and título". `teacher_profile` (02 §4.2) has `credentials_verified_at` but no evidence field, no vetting artefact, and nothing that records who verified what. 04 §9 concedes minors are broadly present ("Moderation liability with minors on the platform") while designing no safeguarding for the 1:1 sessions it does ship.

*Fix:* Week 0, before any teacher touches a student: (1) a vetting file per teacher — certificado de antecedentes penales, título + SENESCYT registration, ACESS registration for the therapist — stored as evidence keys against `teacher_profile` with a verifier name and date, and a hard DB/authz gate that no `booking` can be created for a teacher whose vetting is unverified; (2) a signed code of conduct covering 1:1 sessions with minors, including a prohibition on off-platform private contact with a minor (which directly collides with the WhatsApp-first strategy — resolve it explicitly); (3) a stated policy on guardian presence/visibility for every minor session; (4) a named reporting path with a real escalation route and a `safeguarding_incident` table; (5) name the trade-off the plans currently duck: 'no recording' is the cheapest LOPDP posture AND the removal of the only safeguarding evidence trail. Decide it consciously and write the decision down.

**Live-session access control** — The meeting link is a permanent, unauthenticated bearer URL with no expiry, no per-student binding, no revocation and no attendance control. Anyone who has ever received it — an ex-student, a refunded student, a sibling, anyone in a WhatsApp group it was forwarded to — can rejoin a live class with children in it, indefinitely. No plan states who admits participants to the room.

*Evidence:* 02 §4.5 `booking.meeting_url TEXT, -- Google Meet link; v1 is a link, not an embed`; 01 §5 "Live classes: a URL in a column"; 03 §6 "`sessions.meeting_url` in D1, rendered behind auth". Rendering behind auth protects the *page*, not the *link*. `enrollment.status` includes `'revoked'` and `booking.status` includes `'cancelled'`, but nothing in any plan invalidates the meeting URL on either transition. Google Meet rooms created from a personal account are reusable and outside the platform's control; no plan specifies host-admit, lobby, or a per-session unique link.

*Fix:* One freshly generated link per session (never a personal/reusable room), written at booking-confirmation time and nulled on cancel/refund/revoke; host-admit mandatory and stated in the teacher onboarding doc; server-side attendance marked by the teacher in `attendance` rather than inferred; and a rule that the link is only ever rendered inside the authenticated session page, never emailed or sent over WhatsApp.

**Minors' data outside the therapy vertical** — Every consent, guardian and minor control in the entire plan set is scoped to the therapy vertical. But minors are the core audience of at least three of the four verticals, and the registration flow has no age question at all. A 12-year-old signs up for English or music, the platform processes a minor's personal data with no guardian involvement, no lawful basis captured, no minimum age defined, and no `is_minor` ever set.

*Evidence:* 04 personas include "a Cuenca teenager whose mother pays"; 05 targets school-age queries ("nivelación matemáticas Quito", "suficiencia de inglés"); 04 §9 states outright "Moderation liability with minors on the platform". Yet the ONLY consent gate anywhere is 04 flow (c) step 4, inside the therapy booking flow: "Consent gate — blocking, two steps… (Norma Técnica de Telesalud §7.2.2)". 02 §4.1 defines `is_minor INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` and `birthdate TEXT` on `user`, but no flow in any of the six documents ever populates them: 01's `/registro/` route and 04's `/registro` screen collect name/email/password. `guardian_link` is only exercised via the therapy path. grep for `edad de consentimiento` / `menor de edad`: zero hits.

*Fix:* Decide and document a minimum self-registration age; ask date of birth at registration in every vertical; derive minor status rather than storing it; for any minor, route into a guardian-consent flow (a lighter one than telesalud, but a real one, with consent text version + timestamp + who granted it) before enrolment is granted; and record the lawful basis for every account, not just clinical ones. This is a week-1 change to migration 0001/0002, not a v2 item.

**Clinical data isolation is claimed but does not exist** — The plan's headline protection for children's therapy records — a separate D1 database with a separate binding — is presented as a platform-enforced boundary with "zero blast radius". It is not. Cloudflare bindings are per-Worker, not per-route. Astro compiles to a single Worker, so `env.CLINICAL_DB` and the AES-GCM decryption key are reachable from every handler in that Worker, including the anonymous `/pago/retorno` route and every marketing SSR path. The isolation is a lint rule, and the plan states it as an architectural guarantee.

*Evidence:* 02 §6.2: "**Separate database, separate binding.** `escuela-clinico` is bound only to routes under `/app/terapia/*`. Blast radius of a bug in the course code is zero." Contrast with 03 §2's single-Worker config: `"main": "@astrojs/cloudflare/entrypoints/server"` with `d1_databases` declared at Worker scope. The only enforcement offered is 02 §5 Layer 2: "`scripts/verify-db-boundary.mjs` fails the build if … `CLINICAL_DB` appears outside `src/server/clinical/`" — a grep. 02 §6.3 concedes the encryption limitation ("the Worker can decrypt") but not that the *binding* limitation makes it the same Worker that serves anonymous traffic.

*Fix:* 01 already provisions three Workers. Move clinical to a fourth (or into `escuela-jobs`-style isolation): a dedicated Worker that alone holds `CLINICAL_DB` and the encryption key, reached from the web Worker only over a service binding with a narrow RPC surface, doing its own actor check and writing `clinical_access_log` at its own boundary. Then the blast-radius claim becomes true. Also restate the encryption caveat honestly: the founder holds the Cloudflare account and can deploy code that decrypts, so 'admin cannot read clinical notes' (02 §6.4) is a control against accident and support staff, not against the operator — say that to the DPD before they find it.

**Authorization — the first IDOR is the guardian link** — The `representante` actor carries `wardIds: string[]` and every guardian-scoped query trusts it. Nothing in any plan describes how a `guardian_link` row is created or proven, the scope function does not filter on `revoked_at IS NULL` or `verified_at IS NOT NULL`, and `verified_at` is nullable with no defined verifier. Attack: register an account, insert a guardian link to an arbitrary `user_id` (or accept an unverified self-asserted link at signup), and read that child's progress, bookings and — once the clinical tables deploy — their session notes.

*Evidence:* 02 §5: `| { kind: 'representante'; userId: string; wardIds: string[] }` and `case 'representante': … return inArray(lessonProgress.userId, actor.wardIds);` — no revocation or verification predicate. The revocation filter exists only in the *index* definitions (`CREATE INDEX idx_guardian_by_guardian ON guardian_link(guardian_user_id) WHERE revoked_at IS NULL`), which enforces nothing. `guardian_link.verified_at INTEGER` is nullable and no document says who sets it or on what evidence. 02 open question 3 admits the linking model itself is undecided.

*Fix:* Guardian linking must be an invite-and-accept flow (token to the ward's or the platform's verified contact) or an admin verification with recorded evidence — never a self-asserted id. Derive `wardIds` server-side per request from `guardian_link WHERE revoked_at IS NULL AND verified_at IS NOT NULL`, never from anything in the session payload. Add the cross-guardian isolation case to `tests/isolation.spec.ts` as a launch gate.

**Compliance delivery — no owner, no budget, no calendar** — Five of six documents name the same set of legal preconditions as launch-blocking, and not one of them appears on any schedule, in any budget, or against any name. The 8-week design plan contains zero legal deliverables. The mandatory DPIA is described as mandatory in two files and scheduled in none. The privacy policy, terms, refund policy and minors'-consent page are marked MVP-complete as *routes* with no author.

*Evidence:* 04 §11 week-by-week table (weeks 1–8) contains no legal line item; 04 §2 lists `/legal/terminos · /legal/privacidad · /legal/consentimiento-menores · /legal/reembolsos` as "MVP ✅ static" with no owner. 03 §9: "**Not included:** the external **Data Protection Delegate**, legally mandatory the moment a minor's data is touched". 01 open Q4: "Who is the contracted external DPD? … needs a name and a budget line." DPIA is called mandatory in 04 §9 and 03 §10 but scheduled nowhere. Also entirely absent across all six files: a registro de actividades de tratamiento, a processor/DPA inventory, an ARCO request workflow and SLA, and any named owner for the RIPD transfer clauses that three files say are required.

*Fix:* Open a legal workstream at week 0 with a named owner and a cash line, and make it gate two irreversible technical steps: (a) no `wrangler d1 create` until jurisdiction is signed off — it is immutable at creation (verified against Cloudflare's Nov-2025 D1 jurisdiction changelog); (b) no minor's record is written until the DPD is contracted and the DPIA is drafted. Budget the legal cost explicitly next to the $23–35/month infra number so the founder is not comparing a real bill to a hidden one.

**Backups of children's records** — The backup design instructs a copy of the database export to a personal consumer Google Drive, and creates the clinical R2 buckets with no jurisdiction — which silently voids the entire stated reason for putting the clinical database in the EU. The claim that the clinical export is safe because 'the columns are ciphertext' is false for most of the export.

*Evidence:* 01 §8: "Gzip → R2 `escuela-backups` → **plus one copy off Cloudflare entirely** (Karel's Google Drive)" — with no carve-out for clinical data. 02 §9: "Clinical backup | Same, `escuela-clinico`, encrypted at rest already (columns are ciphertext) | Nightly | R2 `escuela-backups-clinico/`". But only `*_enc` columns are ciphertext; the same export carries `clinical_case.ward_user_id`, `therapist_id`, `pseudonym`, `status`, `opened_at`, `retention_until`, every `clinical_consent` row with guardian/ward ids and `ip_hash`, and every `session_note.session_date` / `visible_to_guardian` — i.e. the fact that a specific child is in speech therapy, with dates. Neither 01 §4 nor 02 §9 creates any R2 bucket with a jurisdiction, while 02 §6.7 rests the whole EU argument on `--jurisdiction=eu` for the database only (Cloudflare docs confirm R2 jurisdiction is a separate per-bucket setting specified in the binding).

*Fix:* Exclude the clinical database from the off-Cloudflare copy entirely, or encrypt that copy client-side with a key the web Worker never sees. Create `escuela-backups-clinico` with a matching jurisdiction and its own scoped API token. Never place minors' health-adjacent records in a consumer cloud account. And correct the sentence 'encrypted at rest already' — it is misleading in the one place it matters most.

### MAJOR (14)

**Authorization architecture — the guarantee is weaker than advertised** — Two plans propose two different, mutually incompatible isolation mechanisms in two different directories, with two CI greps that each would fail the other's code — and one of the greps can never fire at all. Worse, the central claim ("the compiler guarantees isolation") is false: the exhaustive switch fires when a *role* is added, but nothing forces any caller to apply the scope predicate.

*Evidence:* 01 §2 layer 1: "Every D1 query lives in `src/server/repo/*.ts`" + layer 2: "A grep … fails the build on `env.DB.prepare(` or `.batch(` appearing anywhere outside `src/server/repo/`". 02 §2/§5: "`drizzle(env.DB)` may only be called inside `src/server/db/`" + "fails the build if `drizzle(` or `.prepare(` appears anywhere outside `src/server/db/`". Since 02 mandates Drizzle for all access, the literal string `env.DB.prepare(` will never appear in the codebase, so 01's guard is a permanently green no-op that reads as protection. And `scopeLessonProgress(db, actor)` returns a `SQL` fragment the caller must remember to pass to `.where()` — `db.select().from(lessonProgress).where(eq(lessonProgress.lessonId, id))` compiles cleanly and leaks every student's progress.

*Fix:* One directory, one mechanism. Make the scope non-optional by construction — a `scopedDb(actor)` factory that returns per-table builders with the predicate already applied, so the unscoped table object is not exported at all. Keep the exhaustive switch for role additions, but stop describing it as an isolation guarantee. The real gate is 02 §5 layer 3 (`tests/isolation.spec.ts`); make that a required check with a case per table × per role, including guardian↔guardian and teacher↔teacher.

**Audit logging is not tamper-evident** — Both audit logs — including the clinical access log that is the primary evidence the plans expect to show a regulator — live in the same D1 as the data they audit, writable by the same Worker with the same credentials. 'Append-only' is a SQL comment. D1 has no per-table permissions.

*Evidence:* 02 §4.7: `CREATE TABLE audit_log ( -- append-only. No UPDATE, no DELETE, ever.`; 02 §4.8 `clinical_access_log`; 02 §6.5: "Read logging is mandatory… The SPDP's own stated position after the Dec-2025 fines is that it wants evidence and records, not PDF policies." 01 §8 makes the same design for `system_events` ("Append-only, never deleted"). Nothing in either plan prevents or detects a DELETE, and the audit tables are excluded from soft-delete rules but not from physical deletion.

*Fix:* Cheap and worth it before launch: a `prev_hash` chain column so any deletion or edit is detectable, plus a daily append of the log tail to R2 (ideally an immutable/object-locked bucket or a separate account) and an alert if the chain breaks. Without one of these, the evidentiary value of the log in an SPDP proceeding is close to zero, and it is the founder who will be asked to prove the negative.

**Undeclared processors and PII in telemetry** — Error tracking and request logging will capture minors' identifiers and clinical route parameters and ship them to third parties, and neither appears anywhere in the cross-border transfer analysis, which discusses only Cloudflare. No scrubbing rule, no route exclusion, no processor inventory.

*Evidence:* 01 §8 logs "`{evt, req_id, user_id, ruta, ms, d1_rows_read}`" — `user_id` and `ruta` (which for a therapy route contains case/ward identifiers) on every request. 03 §1 stack table: "**Sentry Developer** (5,000 errors/mo, 1 seat, 30-day retention)", with no `beforeSend` scrub and no exclusion for `/app/terapia/*` or `/representante/*`. Meanwhile 01 §2 rejects Clerk/Auth0 precisely because "every student identity leaves for a third US processor, adding a second international-transfer registration" — the same objection applies to Sentry, Resend, Bunny, Meta/WhatsApp, Google Meet and Dátil, none of which is analysed as a transfer.

*Fix:* Exclude clinical and guardian routes from Sentry entirely (route-level `beforeSend` drop), log a per-session pseudonym rather than `user_id`, scrub query strings, bodies and headers by default. Then build the processor inventory the transfer registration will require: every vendor, what data it sees, what DPA exists, what retention applies. It is a one-page table and it is currently missing across six documents.

**Data-subject rights and retention have a screen but no machinery** — An ARCO rights screen is marked MVP-complete, but nothing behind it exists: no retention schedule for non-clinical personal data, no erasure routine, no defined survivor set, no export/portability format, no request log, no response SLA. Retention is defined only for clinical records.

*Evidence:* 04 §2: "`/mi-cuenta` | Profile, clave, LOPDP data rights (ARCO) | ✅ | ssr". Against that: 02 §3 soft deletes are "on *authored content only*… **Never** on `payment`, `payment_event`, `invoice`, `audit_log`", `user` gets only `status IN ('active','suspended','deleted')`, and 02 §6.6 defines retention exclusively for `clinical_case.retention_until`. grep for `supresi`, `portabilidad`, `eliminar cuenta`, `retenci` across 01–06: zero hits outside the clinical paragraph. 02 §10 schedules no deletion cron in MVP.

*Fix:* Before the screen ships: a retention table (what is kept, how long, why), a documented survivor set for erasure (invoices and audit rows survive with a legal basis; progress, notes, uploads, message logs do not), an executable deletion routine tested against the seed data, a JSON export for portability, and a `dsar_request` table capturing request → response with dates so the SLA is provable. A rights screen that opens a support ticket into a WhatsApp thread is worse than none.

**Communications consent and messaging about minors** — WhatsApp is designated a first-class channel and reminders are pushed to it, but there is no consent capture, no opt-out, and no rule that any communication concerning a minor goes to the legal representative rather than to the child.

*Evidence:* 03 §5 makes WhatsApp the reminder channel ("Reminders go to WhatsApp") and 01 §4 schedules a `Recordatorios` cron into the queue. 02 §4.7 defines `message_log` (a send log) but there is no consent, preference or opt-out table anywhere in the 43-table schema, and `user.phone_e164` is a single column with no indication of whose phone it is. 04 open Q6 treats WhatsApp only as a staffing question ("manual number, or WhatsApp Business API?"), not a consent one.

*Fix:* A `communication_consent` table (channel, purpose, text version, granted_at, revoked_at) written at signup, surfaced and revocable in `/mi-cuenta`, and checked in the send path. Add a hard rule in the send function: if the subject is a minor, the recipient is the verified guardian. This is also a Meta policy requirement for template messaging, so it is not optional even commercially.

**Minors' data pushed into uncontrolled third-party accounts** — Two v1 shortcuts route children's data into accounts the platform neither controls nor can audit: quizzes via a teacher's personal Google Form, and live classes via a teacher's personal Google Meet room. Neither is covered by the privacy notice, a DPA, or the transfer register.

*Evidence:* 04 §9: "Quiz builder / auto-grading | … **a teacher can attach a Google Form in v1**". 01 §5 / 03 §6: the live class is "a Google Meet link stored per session" — created by whom, in whose account, is never stated. 02's `clinical_access_log` audits reads of clinical rows but has no visibility into anything that happens in the teacher's Google account.

*Fix:* Prohibit third-party form embeds on any course a minor can enrol in (a plain text/PDF exercise is a fine v1 substitute). Require Meet links to be created from a platform-controlled Workspace identity, or accept the teacher-account model consciously and name Google as a processor in the notice, with a written instruction to teachers about recording, chat retention and link reuse.

**Unrecognised special-category data outside the clinical perimeter** — Two designed fields carry disability/health signal about children but live in the ordinary database with none of the clinical controls — and one of them has no column in the schema at all, so it will be bolted on ad hoc.

*Evidence:* 04 §6: "**`Modo calma`** — a persisted per-account toggle that removes decorative imagery, disables all animation…" — on a platform whose stated differentiator is "children with learning difficulties", a server-stored per-account flag of this kind is a signal about a child's condition, and it sits in `escuela-core` (`--location=enam`, US soil) outside every §6 control. There is no `user_preference` table in the 43-table schema. Separately, 02 §4.5: `note_public TEXT -- NON-clinical only. See §6.` and 02 §4.2 `student_profile.notes_public TEXT` — free-text fields about a child whose only guard is an SQL comment. Teachers will type clinical observations into them within a month.

*Fix:* Keep accessibility preferences in the client (localStorage) or, if they must persist, treat them with the clinical controls and say so in the notice. Replace `note_public`/`notes_public` with a constrained vocabulary, or drop them; if free text stays, put an explicit in-UI warning above the field and exclude it from every export and guardian-visible surface.

**Purpose creep on the national ID** — The cédula is collected for one narrow legal purpose (the SRI buyer-ID threshold) and then re-used for a completely different purpose — anti-piracy watermarking rendered on screen during lessons. At the plan's own price points this is the default path for every buyer, including parents of children in therapy.

*Evidence:* 02 §4.6 encodes the collection purpose as a constraint: `CHECK (total_cents < 5000 OR (buyer_id_number IS NOT NULL …))`. 01 §5 step 6 then re-uses it: "**Per-student watermark**: a DOM overlay rendering `María Ramírez · ****4821 · 2026-09-06`", repeated in 03 §6 ("a per-student DOM watermark showing name + cédula"). Arithmetic: at the D6 live-class price of $120/month, every single package purchase is ≥ $50, so the ID is collected from essentially every buyer, and the watermark then renders name + ID fragment on a child's screen — visible in any screen-share or classroom projection.

*Fix:* Watermark with an opaque per-enrolment code that maps to the account only in your own database. It is exactly as traceable for anti-piracy, and it removes an identity-document fragment from the screen and from any leaked recording. Also state the finalidad for the cédula in the notice and do not extend it.

**Payments — the stated idempotency key does not exist, and the confirm path is attackable** — Two plans name `UNIQUE(client_transaction_id)` as the entire idempotency guarantee on the money path; the actual schema has no such column. The only unique key available (`provider_payment_id`) is not known until after Confirm, so there is no pre-Confirm order-level lock. Combined with an anonymous redirect handler, a short guessable transaction id, a 30-calls/minute status rate limit and a 5-minute auto-reversal window, an unauthenticated attacker can burn the confirm budget and cause genuine sales to silently reverse.

*Evidence:* 03 §4.5: "Idempotency lives in D1: `UNIQUE(client_transaction_id)` on `payments`"; 01 §6: "`ordenes.payphone_tx_id` carries a `UNIQUE` constraint". Actual DDL (02 §4.6): `payment` has only `UNIQUE (provider, provider_payment_id)`; grep for `client_transaction` in 02 returns zero hits. 03 §4.1 then specifies the id generator: "`clientTransactionId` (**max 15 characters** — use a base36 D1 rowid, not a UUID)" — i.e. sequential and enumerable. 01 route table: "`/pago/retorno` | **SSR** | **anon** | PayPhone redirect landing. Must work without a session." 03 §4.4: "Status GETs are rate-limited to **30 calls/minute**." 01 §6: "if your server does not call `POST /api/button/V2/Confirm` within 5 minutes… PayPhone automatically reverses the transaction."

*Fix:* Add a `client_transaction_id TEXT UNIQUE` column written at Prepare, and generate it from `crypto.getRandomValues` in base36 (15 chars of base36 ≈ 77 bits) — never from a sequential rowid. Rate-limit and Turnstile-gate `/pago/retorno` and the webhook by client transaction id, and serialise Confirm per order so a flood of forged returns cannot consume the 30/min budget during a real customer's five-minute window. Reconcile the three plans on one idempotency design before anyone writes checkout.

**File upload validation** — The design states a '≤5 MB, jpg/png/pdf' rule for payment proofs and a direct-to-R2 presigned upload path — but a presigned PUT cannot enforce a size cap, and no plan specifies content-type pinning, an upload host separate from the app origin, or attachment disposition. Any authenticated student can write arbitrarily large objects, and admin-viewed user-supplied PDFs/SVGs will be served from the media custom domain.

*Evidence:* 01 §6: "Student uploads the comprobante (≤5 MB, jpg/png/pdf) via a presigned R2 PUT"; 04 §12: "Video upload **must** go direct to R2 via presigned URL, bypassing the Worker". Verified against Cloudflare's R2 presigned-URL docs: Content-Type *can* be pinned into the signature, but "`POST` (multipart form uploads via HTML forms) is not currently supported" — so there is no policy mechanism for a maximum object size, and each multipart part is separately signed. No plan mentions serving user uploads from a distinct hostname, `Content-Disposition: attachment`, or banning SVG.

*Fix:* Sign a Worker-chosen key with a pinned `ContentType` and a short expiry; after upload, HEAD the object and reject/delete anything over the limit before it is ever linked; serve user-uploaded files from a separate cookieless hostname with `Content-Disposition: attachment` and no SVG. For comprobantes specifically, just proxy through the Worker — the 128 MB-memory argument in 01 §5 applies to 2 GB videos, not to 5 MB JPEGs, and proxying gives you real validation for free.

**Three incompatible guardian models, and a consent screen that may ship after the bookings it gates** — The architecture, database and design plans each describe a different guardian data model, and they disagree on whether the guardian portal and its consent gate are MVP at all. Under the architecture's sequencing, therapy bookings ship in MVP while the two-step consent screen is v2 — which means collecting minors' data with the consent flow absent.

*Evidence:* 01 §2: "A guardian is a normal `alumno`-role account with rows here [`representantes`]" and the route table marks "`/representante/` … Guardian view: child's progress, consents, session notes. **v2.**", with 01 Phasing cutting "guardian portal, 1:1 booking with availability rules" from MVP. 02 §4.2 models separate `user` accounts joined by `guardian_link`, and 02 open Q3 admits the model is undecided "before 0002 ships". 04 §2 marks "`/representante` … ✅ MVP" and "`/representante/consentimiento/[menor]` | Two-step telesalud consent | ✅ MVP", and 04 line 330 states "the guardian view is a switch, not a second login" — under which there is no ward *user id* for `clinical_case.ward_user_id` / `clinical_consent.ward_user_id` (02 §4.8) to reference.

*Fix:* Pick one model before migration 0002 and propagate it to all three documents. Then make the dependency structural rather than a matter of sequencing luck: the therapy booking route must query the consent record and refuse to create a booking without it, so the two cannot ship apart. If therapy is cut from v1 (which three plans recommend), cut the booking route too, not just the notes.

**Tax and invoicing — the 0% IVA scenario is already dead at the low end of the target** — Three plans carry 'RIMPE Negocio Popular (0% IVA) vs general regime (15%)' as an open question. It is not open: at the founder's own minimum target it is already breached, and the brand plan supplies a second, independent reason the courses are not 0%-rated. Prices, the checkout, and the invoicing integration are all being designed against a scenario that cannot occur.

*Evidence:* Locked decisions D6: "Target the **20–200 student** range" and "At 20 students on a $120/month live-class package that is ~$2,400/month gross". Arithmetic: 20 × $120 × 12 = **$28,800/year**, against the "$20,000/year ceiling" cited in 01 open Q2 and 02 open Q2 — so the ceiling is exceeded by 44% at the *bottom* of the target range, and by 14× at the top. Independently, 06 §8: "Per RALRTI Art. 187 … education services are only 0%-IVA when delivered by an establishment **legally authorised by the State**, and courses from non-recognised institutions are taxed at the general rate." The plans also contradict each other on when invoicing ships: 01 MVP includes "Dátil invoicing from the queue"; 03 §11 puts "Dátil e-invoicing wired into the payment flow" in v2 (weeks 7–16).

*Fix:* Stop treating this as open. Default to 15% IVA-inclusive published prices (`price_point.iva_rate_bp = 1500`, `iva_included = 1`, which the schema already defaults to), and treat RUC + firma electrónica + Dátil as week-0 blocking rather than v2 — you cannot legally take the first sale without them. If the Operador de Capacitación registration is pursued, it changes both the certificate wording and the rate, so make it a dated decision with an owner, not an open question.

**Refunds, chargebacks and consumer rights** — There is a `refund` table and a `revoked` enrolment status, and no flow connecting them, no credit-note instrument despite the plans noting invoices become irreversible, no chargeback handling, and no refund policy content behind a route that is marked MVP-complete.

*Evidence:* 02 §4.6 defines `refund` and `enrollment.status … 'revoked'`; nothing in any of the six documents wires refund → revoke enrolment → invalidate media tokens. 01 §6 flags the trap without closing it: "consumidor-final invoices are **irreversible once transmitted**" — and `invoice.sri_estado` allows `'anulado'` but no nota de crédito entity exists. grep across 01–06 for `chargeback`, `contracargo`, `devolución`, `desistimiento`: zero hits. `/legal/reembolsos` is listed as MVP static with no author.

*Fix:* Write the refund policy first (including whatever distance-selling withdrawal right applies to digitally delivered services in Ecuador — confirm with counsel, do not guess), then model it: refund → revoke enrolment → issue the correct SRI instrument → invalidate outstanding media tokens and meeting links. Add a chargeback/dispute state to `payment` and decide who eats the loss when a teacher has already been paid out.

**Teacher payouts — classification and withholding are entirely absent** — The plans pay teachers a 70% revenue share by hand, from a spreadsheet, with no contract, no invoice requirement, no withholding fields and no analysis of dependent-employment risk — while the platform sets the price, owns the customer, controls the schedule and brands the service.

*Evidence:* 02 §4.2 `revenue_share_bp INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 7000` with 02 open Q4 admitting it is "a placeholder"; 02 §4.6 `teacher_payout` carries `platform_fee_cents` and `gateway_fee_cents` but no withholding fields and no reference to a teacher invoice; `teacher_profile.payout_id_number TEXT -- cédula/RUC, required to pay them` is the entire treatment. 01 Phasing: "teacher payouts (Karel pays teachers by hand… a spreadsheet is fine at three teachers)". grep across all six files for `IESS`, `dependencia`, `contratista`, `honorarios`, `retención`: zero hits.

*Fix:* Before the first sale: a written services contract per teacher; a decision and a written record of who is merchant of record; a requirement that each teacher issues an invoice (or that you can legally issue the substitute document if they have no RUC); withholding fields on `teacher_payout`; and an accountant's read on dependency risk given that you set price, schedule and brand. Retroactive reclassification with back-contributions is the kind of bill that ends a bootstrap.

### MINOR (4)

**Auth hardening for accounts that read children's data** — No second factor anywhere, for any role — including therapists and admins who can read minors' records — plus long-lived auto-extending sessions on devices the design itself says are shared within a household, and no device-management surface in MVP.

*Evidence:* grep across 01–06 for `2FA`, `MFA`, `TOTP`, `segundo factor`: zero hits. 01 §2: "**Session policy:** 30-day expiry" and Cloudflare Access (a second factor for staff) is explicitly "Rejected for students, viable for staff" but never actually adopted. 04 §6: "No time limits anywhere… Auth sessions warn and extend rather than drop", against 04's own persona of a household sharing one phone. Concurrent-session eviction exists (01 §5 step 3) but only as anti-piracy, with no user-facing device list.

*Fix:* Better Auth ships TOTP — make it mandatory for `profesor`, `terapeuta` and `admin`, which is a few hours of work and removes the worst credential-theft outcome. Revoke all sessions on password change and on role change. Keep the 'never lose work' UX for students, but not for accounts that can read another family's data.

**Media token scope and lifetime** — The media-token endpoint is grouped with the payment webhook as a route that must accept foreign POSTs, and the token it mints is a transferable bearer credential valid for six hours. This is the single most likely place the enrolment check gets skipped, and it duplicates a check that also lives in the lesson page.

*Evidence:* 01 §1: "no hand-rolled `/api/*` routes except three that must accept foreign POSTs: `/api/webhooks/payphone`, `/api/auth/[...all]`, `/api/media/token`". 01 §5 step 4: "Worker mints an **embed-view token**, `expires = now + 6h`", while step 2 performs the enrolment check in the SSR page — two paths, one check documented. The 6-hour window is also conceded in the same section: "Accept as unavoidable: … manifest extraction inside the 6-hour window".

*Fix:* Make token minting an Astro Action or a session-gated POST with origin checking — it has no reason to accept foreign POSTs. Scope the token to one lesson for one enrolment, and set TTL to lesson duration + a small margin rather than six hours. Route the enrolment check through the same repo function the lesson page uses, so there is exactly one implementation.

**Internal contradictions that will decide security behaviour by accident** — Several load-bearing details are specified differently in different plans, and whichever file the implementer opens first will decide the outcome. These are cheap to reconcile now and expensive later.

*Evidence:* (a) Password hashing: 01 §2 and 03 §3 both mandate overriding Better Auth with hand-written `node:crypto.scryptSync`, while 02 §11 says the opposite — "Use Better Auth's scrypt as shipped; do not hand-roll a weaker KDF to save CPU". 03's `verify` is left unwritten as `/* parse params, re-derive, timingSafeEqual */`, and `timingSafeEqual` throws on length mismatch, turning a malformed stored hash into a 500. (I verified against Cloudflare's docs that `node:crypto` is fully supported on Workers, so both options work — but only one can be built.) (b) Video upload destination: 04 §12 says "direct to R2 via presigned URL" is "a hard design requirement"; 01 §5 and 03 §6 say direct to **Bunny** over TUS. (c) Table naming: 01 names `usuarios/cursos/matriculas/ordenes`, 02 ships `user/course/enrollment/"order"`. (d) 05 requires three D1 tables — `eventos_web`, `revisiones_contenido`, and sitemap `lastmod` fields — that do not exist anywhere in 02's 43-table schema. (e) `user.is_minor` is a stored boolean that goes stale the day a child turns 18, and any future authz predicate reading it will be silently wrong.

*Fix:* One reconciliation pass before week 1: pick one hashing approach and write the verify function with tests; pick one upload destination; pick one naming convention; add the SEO tables to migration 0007; derive minor status from `birthdate` at read time instead of storing it.

**Provenance of every legal claim in the plan set** — The entire compliance design rests on specific article numbers, resolution codes, fine amounts and a technical norm that none of the six documents sources beyond 'the research digest', which is not in the repository. The consent flow, the DPD requirement, the jurisdiction decision and the certificate wording are all built directly on these citations.

*Evidence:* 02 closing line: "The Ecuadorian legal interpretations are inherited from the research digest and require a local data-protection lawyer and tax accountant before launch." 06 §2 likewise: "Per the research digest, terapia de lenguaje in Ecuador is a regulated health activity…". Cited as fact across the set and unsourced: LOPDP Art. 25 / Art. 31.1 / 31.2, Resolución SPDP-SPD-2026-0004-R, Res. SPDP-SPD-2025-0028-R Arts. 10.1 and 15, Norma Técnica de Telesalud §7.2.2 (RO 2º Sup. 153, 28-oct-2025), RALRTI Art. 187, and two fine figures (USD 259,644.01 / 194,856.16). I could not verify any of them in this session (web search budget exhausted). Note the contrast: every Cloudflare-platform claim I *could* check held up — D1 `--jurisdiction eu|fedramp` set only at creation and immutable, full `node:crypto` support under `nodejs_compat`, presigned-URL Content-Type pinning with no size-policy mechanism.

*Fix:* Hand the legal citation list to Ecuadorian counsel as a checklist and get each one confirmed with a document reference, before the consent texts are written and before `d1 create` fixes the jurisdiction permanently. Anything that comes back wrong invalidates a screen, a schema decision, or a price — all of which are cheap this week and expensive in month three.

## Business viability, pricing, does it sell

**Verdict.** The software is buildable — over-scoped by roughly 2x, but buildable. The business is not specified at all, and that is the real verdict. Six documents totalling ~6,000 lines contain exactly one financial table, and it is an infrastructure bill; there is no price the six plans agree on (the design says $29/mes, the founder's own locked decisions and the tech-stack cost model say $120/month — a 4.1x swing on gross revenue at the target scale), no confirmed teacher revenue split (70% is a schema default flagged "confirm the real number"), no contribution margin, no break-even, and no teacher-capacity model. When I ran the arithmetic the plans skip: at $120/mo IVA-inclusive with a 70/30 split and PayPhone's 5.75%, the platform keeps $24.40/student/month, so D6's 20-student milestone is ~$488/month of contribution before the legally mandatory external DPD retainer that the cost table explicitly excludes; at the design's $29/mes it is $65/month, less than the infrastructure bill. Three teachers cannot physically serve the 200-student upper target on a live 1:1 model (~258 billable hours/month = 18–64 students), and the schema's `uq_booking_teacher_slot` unique index makes group classes — the only escape — impossible without a migration. Three structural gaps will bite in month three regardless of code quality: the "/mes" product has no recurring-billing mechanism anywhere in the stack, so every student must be manually re-sold each month; English is a full launch vertical with an SEO pillar and four launch articles but no teacher among the three; and the architecture plan cuts 1:1 booking to v2 while the design and database plans ship it as MVP — meaning the MVP as scheduled sells the recorded catalogue (unproven demand) and cannot sell the live packages the founder's own research says convert. My concrete call: v1 is the live rail only, one vertical, one teacher — música with Andrés at ~$120/month, the one combination where the founder's own $2,400/month arithmetic is actually reachable — with terapia as the fast follower the moment the ACESS permiso is confirmed, programación cut (the SEO plan already concedes that SERP to Platzi and YouTube), inglés cut until a teacher exists, and D4's authoring studio moved to weeks 9–16, after there is revenue to author into. That cut removes ~17 of the 39 MVP screens and is the only one available that respects D8's "cut features, never craft".

## What is missing entirely

**Verdict.** No. The infrastructure half of this bundle is unusually good and largely buildable; the business half is not specified at all, and the 6-8 week MVP is not achievable as written. The plans cover rendering, auth, schema, payments-in and video with real rigour, then stop at the point where a real customer touches the business: there is no refund process, no renewal mechanism, no admin tooling beyond six route names, no buyer/beneficiary split, no support model, no content-production estimate, and no product instrumentation. Roughly a third of the operational surface has no owner in any document, and — worse — the three technical plans quietly disagree about whether the thing that actually earns money (live classes) is in the MVP at all: design says usable by week 3, tech-stack says v2, architecture cuts it. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT THING THIS BUNDLE GETS WRONG: every plan states that live monthly class packages are how revenue starts, and not one of them models a live class. There is no session or cohort entity, no capacity column anywhere in a 50-table schema, no attendance screen, no package balance, and no renewal billing - while the one scheduling constraint that does exist (uq_booking_teacher_slot) makes a group class physically impossible to store. Meanwhile roughly three of eight weeks go to the recorded-course authoring studio. That is the whole build budget aimed at the product that does not pay the bills yet, on top of a schema that cannot represent the one that does. Fix that before writing a line of code; everything else on this list is cheaper than it.
